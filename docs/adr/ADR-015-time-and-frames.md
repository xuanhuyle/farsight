# ADR-015 — Time scales, epoch representation, reference frames and aberration correction
**Status:** Accepted 2026-08-28 (MVP commitment; see Revisit triggers)
**Date:** 2026-08-26
**Deciders:** FarSight founding engineering
**Plan reference:** FARSIGHT_FOUNDATION_PLAN.md §14 item 3 (frame and time-system errors are the domain's most common silent killers; the SPICE-vs-astropy cross-check), §5 (SPICE ground truth), §7 (no JSON floats in hashed documents), §8 (boundary-typed, raw SI core), §17 (weeks 1-2 exit gate), §18 (AT-11); decision D6
**Related ADRs:** ADR-001 (no JSON float may appear in a hashed document, which is what rules out a raw ET double and forces the representation below), ADR-016 (the LSK that authorizes every time conversion, and the PCK and FK that realize every non-inertial frame, are hash-pinned kernels decided there), ADR-003 (`SimTime` in `run_to` and `state` is the in-memory alias defined here), ADR-006 (a tier claim's comparison epochs are `Epoch` documents under this record, not the UTC strings its example spells), ADR-008 (the boundary-typed raw-core rule, and the astropy dependency the independent cross-check rests on), ADR-010 (fault trigger epochs and elapsed-time offsets are reconciled here), ADR-018 (the geometry stage that issues the requests this record shapes), ADR-020 (a channel's sample grid is a sequence of these epochs, which is how a sample index becomes a time), ADR-021 (referent epochs are converted once, at import, under this record's rules), ADR-007 (the derived epoch-label index is package content), ADR-012 (astropy's IERS auto-download is a network path in a base-install dependency and is switched off here), ADR-000 (the escape-hatch tokens and the `review_signoffs` field TIME-1 lands in)

## Context

The forcing question arrives in hour one of the week 1-2 geometry service and has no default answer: **what is an epoch inside a hashed FarSight document, and which geometry conventions are hashed spec content rather than an adapter's opinion?**

The set currently answers it four incompatible ways. ADR-003's `run_to(t: SimTime)` and `state(target, et, frame, observer)` use an undefined `SimTime`. ADR-010 writes `t: EpochQuantity`, also undefined, and a separate `fire_epoch_s` in seconds from scenario start. ADR-006's tier-claim example spells comparison epochs as `"2024-04-08T00:00:00Z"`. Nothing reconciles them, and two of the four cannot be made to work: a raw float `et` is forbidden in a hashed document by ADR-001 rule 2, and a UTC string is lossy in a way that matters. UTC is not a uniform timescale. Converting it to the TDB seconds CSPICE actually consumes requires a leap-second kernel; the string `23:59:60` is a legal UTC time with no counterpart in any uniform scale; and an epoch authored today in UTC for a date two years out is converted against a leap-second table that may acquire another entry before that date arrives. That last property is fatal for a content-addressed system in a specific way: the bytes of the frozen document would not change while its *meaning* did.

Two more conventions are undecided and each is load-bearing at the same tolerances the acceptance tests are written at. The reference frame — the inertial frame for states, and the Earth body-fixed frame the ground stations sit in — is the input to AT-11's elevation cross-check, and the two candidate body-fixed frames are not interchangeable: `IAU_EARTH` is a low-precision rotation model and `ITRF93` is the high-precision realization driven by a binary Earth-orientation kernel. And the aberration correction (`NONE`, `LT`, `LT+S`, `CN+S` and their transmission counterparts) decides whether a range is the instantaneous geometric separation or the distance the photons actually travelled. At the flagship's ranges the light time is of order twenty minutes and the target moves a long way during it; the resulting difference in range is orders of magnitude larger than AT-11's 10 km tolerance. The exact figure is **UNVERIFIED — confirm at implementation time**, and Enforcement item 4 makes computing and logging it a week-1 task rather than a sentence in this record.

What breaks is not a crash. Plan §14 item 3 names this class precisely: frame and time-system errors are the domain's most common silent killers, and the SPICE-versus-astropy cross-check is the only thing in the plan that catches them. A wrong aberration convention produces plausible numbers that pass every hash check, satisfy every schema, and are found by the week-8 external auditor or by a domain reviewer in front of a customer (§19 risk 2). And because epoch strings sit inside `spec_hash`, therefore inside `experiment_hash`, therefore inside every root hash, changing the representation after week 3 invalidates every archived golden and every published pre-registration hash — §16 says the identity scheme is effectively permanent once customers hold packages.

## Decision

**1. The canonical epoch in every hashed document is TDB seconds past J2000, carried in one type.**

```python
class Epoch(BaseModel, frozen=True, extra="forbid"):
    scale: Literal["TDB"]                 # single-member enum in the MVP; see decision 3
    seconds_past_j2000: Quantity          # unit "s", decimal-string magnitude (ADR-001 rule 2)
```

TDB seconds past J2000 is what CSPICE consumes and returns, so the authoritative value crosses no conversion on its way into a geometry call. The magnitude is a decimal string under ADR-001's grammar, like every other physical quantity in the system, because an epoch **is** a physical quantity and a carve-out would be the first crack in a rule whose whole value is that it has none. There is exactly one epoch type; `EpochQuantity` (ADR-010) is retired in favour of it.

**2. Durations are a separate type and are never epochs.**

```python
class Duration(BaseModel, frozen=True, extra="forbid"):
    seconds: Quantity                     # unit "s"; an interval on the TDB timeline
    anchor: Ref | None                    # None means the ScenarioTemplate's epoch_start
```

ADR-010's `fire_epoch_s` and `cleared_epoch_s`, described as SI seconds from scenario start, are `Duration`s under this rule, and their anchor is the scenario's `epoch_start: Epoch`. A `Duration` added to an `Epoch` is a TDB-timeline interval, not proper time; the TDB-versus-TT rate difference is periodic and bounded far below any tolerance in this MVP, and its exact bound is **UNVERIFIED — confirm at implementation time**. The distinction is enforced at the type level because "seconds since the start" and "seconds past J2000" are both `Quantity{unit: "s"}` and nothing but a type stops one being assigned to the other.

**3. `scale` is a single-member enum, and UTC is an input and a display spelling, never a stored scale.** TT, TAI, UT1 and UTC exist at the boundaries and nowhere else. Authoring may use a UTC literal at draft stage. `freeze` converts it once, through the LSK pinned in the design's `KernelSet` (ADR-016), and records the conversion in the object's **unhashed provenance block** (ADR-001 rule 4):

```json
"provenance": {"epoch_conversions": [
  {"field": "scenario.epoch_start", "from_scale": "UTC",
   "literal": "2024-04-08T00:00:00Z", "lsk_sha256": "..."}]}
```

The conversion record is deliberately outside the hash. Inside it, two authors who wrote the same instant in different spellings would produce different objects, and ADR-001 already pays that dedup cost once for significant figures; paying it a second time for authoring history buys nothing. The frozen TDB number is the record. `farsight evidence verify` re-performs each recorded conversion when the `spice` extra is present and reports a mismatch as a provenance discrepancy naming the field; with no extras it checks that the `lsk_sha256` resolves and reports `epoch_conversions: unchecked (spice extra absent)`. It never silently re-maps a frozen value.

**4. Human-readable labels are derived data with declared provenance, never inputs.** The package gains `experiment/epoch_labels.json`: a map from each `Epoch` in the package to its UTC and TDB calendar spellings, plus the `lsk_sha256` and the CSPICE and SpiceyPy versions that produced them. `report/summary.md` renders from this file, which is what keeps ADR-007's byte-equal re-render rule satisfiable on a zero-extras install — the renderer reads a string rather than performing a conversion it has no kernel for. The file is derived: nothing consumes it, and regenerating it under a different LSK changes a label without changing any `Epoch`.

**5. Frames are explicit, hashed, and named per request.** No frame is ever inherited from an adapter default.

- **Inertial:** `J2000`, as the frame string passed to SPICE. What realizes those axes is whatever the furnished kernels define, and that realization is pinned by kernel hash (ADR-016) rather than asserted here. The residual rotation between the FK5/J2000 dynamical frame and the ICRF is far below every tolerance in this MVP; the figure is **UNVERIFIED — confirm at implementation time** and is one of the deltas Enforcement item 4 logs.
- **Earth body-fixed:** `ITRF93`, not `IAU_EARTH`. `IAU_EARTH` is a low-precision rotation model; `ITRF93` is realized by a binary Earth-orientation PCK, which is where Earth-orientation data enters FarSight — as a hash-pinned kernel, not as an ambient table. The two frames differ by an amount that is large compared with AT-11's 0.01 degree elevation tolerance; the figure is **UNVERIFIED** and Enforcement item 4 measures it in week 1 by computing the same station elevation both ways and logging the delta.
- **Station topocentric:** the frame named by the station's frames kernel (FK). Whether the NAIF PDS4 station kernel set ships an FK defining a topocentric frame for each of the DSOC ground stations is **UNVERIFIED — confirm at implementation time**. If it does not, FarSight authors a text FK, which is a new kernel attributed to FarSight under ADR-016's attribution rules, not a modified NAIF kernel — and it is hash-pinned like any other.

**6. Aberration correction is mandatory hashed spec content with no default anywhere in the code path.**

```python
Aberration = Literal["NONE", "LT", "LT+S", "CN", "CN+S",
                     "XLT", "XLT+S", "XCN", "XCN+S"]   # reception, then transmission

class GeometryRequest(BaseModel, frozen=True, extra="forbid"):
    target: str                  # NAIF name or integer id as a string
    observer: str
    frame: str                   # "J2000" | "ITRF93" | "<STATION>_TOPO"
    aberration: Aberration       # NO DEFAULT: omission is a validation error
    quantity_class: Literal["range", "light_time", "direction", "state"]
    epochs: Ref                  # the sample grid (ADR-020)
    rationale: str | None        # required by the consistency rule below
```

Two rules make the field meaningful rather than merely present. First, **omission is a validation error, not a fallback** — the same discipline AT-6 imposes on unknown parameters, applied to conventions. Second, a mechanical consistency rule: stellar aberration displaces an apparent *direction* and does not change a range, so a `range` or `light_time` request carrying a `+S` member is rejected at construction, and a `direction` request carrying `NONE` or a non-`+S` member is rejected unless `rationale` is non-empty.

The conventions the flagship scenario templates declare, recorded here so that they are arguable rather than accidental: ranges, one-way light times and free-space path terms use the converged-Newtonian reception case `CN`, because the link budget depends on the distance the photons travelled; apparent directions — station elevation and azimuth, pointing offsets, Sun-probe-Earth angle — use `CN+S`; the `X`-prefixed members are the transmission case and are used when the near end emits. `NONE` is admissible only for a quantity explicitly labelled geometric, and that label travels in the channel metadata (ADR-020). Where a request exists to reproduce a golden, its aberration member must match the convention stated in the archived source: the Horizons response is a hash-pinned `DataArtifact` (plan §14 item 5) and its stated settings are read from the artifact, never copied into prose here.

**7. Two conversion implementations exist by design, and they are cross-checked rather than merged.** The authoritative path for anything entering a hashed document is SPICE, in `farsight/engines/spice/time.py`, under the pinned LSK. The independent path is astropy, in `farsight/units/time.py`, which is where ADR-008's contract already permits astropy and which runs on the zero-extras base install. Plan §14 item 3 asks for exactly this independence and it is the only mechanism that catches a systematic frame or time-system error, so the two are never collapsed into one call.

One consequence of astropy being a base-install dependency is a network path we must close explicitly: `farsight.units.time` sets `astropy.utils.iers.conf.auto_download = False` and `auto_max_age = None` at import, and **UT1 is not used anywhere in the MVP** — Earth orientation enters only through the binary PCK of decision 5. Without this, a base-install dependency would attempt an IERS fetch and ADR-012's structural no-network claim would be false on the Windows leg, where the network-namespace check does not exist.

**8. `SimTime` is defined as an in-memory alias and never appears in a hashed document.**

```python
SimTime = Annotated[float, Unit("s")]   # TDB seconds past J2000, in-memory only
```

This is ADR-008's boundary-typed raw-core rule applied to time: `Epoch` is the hashed wire form, `SimTime` is the float64 the engine boundary takes, and `Epoch.seconds_past_j2000.to_si()` is the single conversion point. ADR-003's `run_to(t: SimTime)` is correct as written. Its `state(target, observer, frame, et)` is not: it has no parameter able to carry `aberration`, so an adapter honouring it would have to supply the default this record's Option 7 rejects. `state` takes a `GeometryRequest` plus a `SimTime`; ADR-003's signature moves.

## Options considered

### Option 1 — UTC ISO 8601 strings as the canonical hashed epoch — REJECTED
The strongest form of this is not naive. UTC strings are what every referent source publishes, what every operator reads, what ADR-006's example already spells, and what a stranger opening `runspec_4242.json` in a text editor can check against a published pass table without any tooling — which is a real contribution to the two-hour audit. They sort lexicographically and they carry their own scale. Rejected because the mapping from a UTC string to the uniform scale CSPICE consumes is not a function of the string: it needs a leap-second table, so the document is not self-contained, and `23:59:60` is a legal UTC time with no counterpart in TDB. Decisively, an epoch authored for a future date is converted against a table that may gain an entry later, which means the frozen document's meaning could change while its bytes did not. For a content-addressed system that is not a rounding concern, it is a contradiction. The auditor-legibility benefit is recovered in full by decision 4's derived label index.

### Option 2 — An exact integer count of sub-second units past J2000, as a JSON integer — REJECTED
This is the technically cleanest option and it deserves a real hearing. ADR-001 already permits exact-range JSON integers; an integer picosecond or nanosecond count is exactly representable, orders without parsing, admits exact interval arithmetic with no rounding anywhere, and removes the decimal grammar from the hot path of every epoch. Rejected on two grounds. It is a carve-out in ADR-001 rule 2, whose enumeration of permitted integers is indices, counts, exponents and array shapes — deliberately not physical quantities — and the rule's value is that it has no exceptions to argue about. And the exactness does not survive the boundary: CSPICE takes a double, so the integer is converted to a float64 on every call and the exactness is spent immediately. A single float64 of seconds past J2000 resolves the benchmark span far below any tolerance here, and the decimal string round-trips to the same double, which Enforcement item 1 tests to 1 ULP.

### Option 3 — Julian date or MJD as a decimal string — REJECTED
The standard astronomical interchange format, astropy-native, and it avoids committing to a J2000 offset convention. Rejected because it does not solve the problem it appears to: a Julian date still needs a scale attached, so the `scale` field survives unchanged, and the magnitude carries about seven leading digits of day count that buy nothing while eroding sub-second headroom at the double boundary. SPICE consumes ET seconds, so every call would convert.

### Option 4 — Two-part epoch, integer day plus decimal seconds-of-day (the astropy `Time` internal design) — REJECTED
Astropy splits a time into two float64 precisely to keep sub-nanosecond precision over centuries, and copying a design that a mature library adopted for good reasons is usually right. Rejected because our precision requirement is set by AT-11's 10 km and 0.01 degree tolerances, which a single float64 of seconds past J2000 clears with orders of magnitude to spare over the benchmark span, and because SPICE's own API takes a single double — so a two-part representation would collapse to one double at every boundary while doubling the number of fields every schema, validator and golden has to carry.

### Option 5 — `Epoch{scale: "TDB", seconds_past_j2000: Quantity}`, with explicit frames and a mandatory aberration field — CHOSEN
One epoch type, one scale, the same decimal-string quantity representation as everything else in the system, no conversion between the hashed form and what SPICE consumes, and every convention that changes a number is a required field in a hashed document rather than a default in an adapter.

### Option 6 — `IAU_EARTH` as the station body-fixed frame — REJECTED
Genuinely attractive for the MVP: `IAU_EARTH` needs only a text PCK, which is small, human-readable, ships with every toolkit, and has no coverage window to check at freeze — it would remove an entire class of ADR-016 machinery from the week-1 path. Rejected because the station elevation cross-check (AT-11, plan §14 item 3) is one of the two anchors that make the geometry trustworthy at all, and running it against a low-precision rotation model means the cross-check's residual is dominated by a frame choice we made rather than by the defect class it exists to catch. A test whose noise floor is our own convention error cannot detect a convention error.

### Option 7 — Aberration correction as an adapter default or a runtime configuration key — REJECTED
This is what almost every SPICE-using codebase does: a constant near the top of the geometry module, occasionally a config key, and it works fine for a team that has agreed on the convention once. It is also cheaper, keeps `GeometryRequest` smaller, and avoids asking a scenario author to make a choice they may not be equipped to make. Rejected because a default is invisible in the evidence package: the number changes, the hash changes, and nothing in the package says why. FarSight's product claim is that the causal inputs of a run are inside its `spec_hash`; an adapter default that moves a range by more than the acceptance tolerance is a causal input that is not. Author-facing difficulty is answered by scenario templates that declare the conventions once, not by a fallback.

## Consequences

**Buys us:** an epoch that means one thing everywhere in the system and needs no conversion to reach the geometry engine; a hashed record in which every convention that can move a number is a required field, so an aberration or frame mistake is visible in a diff rather than buried in a module; the SPICE-versus-astropy cross-check as an executable comparison of two independent implementations rather than an aspiration; and Earth orientation entering as a hash-pinned kernel instead of an ambient table, which is what lets a Tier-A replay mean anything about geometry.

**Costs us:** authoring friction that lands on exactly the people least equipped to absorb it — a scenario author must now state a frame and an aberration correction per geometry request, and the consistency rule will reject requests that a working engineer considers obviously fine. Two time implementations means two things to keep pinned and one cross-check to keep green, and when it goes red the diagnosis is genuinely hard, because either implementation could be right. The derived label index is a package file whose provenance must be explained. And freezing a design authored in UTC requires the `spice` extra, so the authoring path and the auditing path have different installs.

**Forecloses:** UT1-dependent precision, permanently for the MVP. Earth orientation is whatever the binary PCK realizes; we cannot ingest an EOP table, because an unhashed ambient input is exactly what this record exists to prevent, so any customer needing products at the precision where UT1 and polar motion are the limiting terms is unserved and must be told so.

It forecloses honest forward-dated UTC authoring. An epoch authored in UTC for a date beyond the pinned LSK's leap-second table is frozen against a table that may later be wrong, and we will never silently re-map it — so a mission-planning use case that schedules a pass years ahead in UTC carries a permanent, disclosed error of up to the number of leap seconds announced in between. `verify` reports the discrepancy; nothing corrects it, because correcting it would change a frozen document.

And it forecloses spacecraft-clock time as a first-class epoch. A datum timestamped in SCLK must be converted at import through an SCLK kernel, and if no SCLK kernel exists for that spacecraft the datum cannot enter as an `Epoch` at all — it can only be carried as an uninterpreted label on a `DataArtifact`. For a product whose referents are other people's telemetry, that is a real intake restriction.

## Enforcement

1. **`test_epoch_representation`** (unit tier, Windows and Linux, every commit; **first green by week 1**): walks every Pydantic model in `farsight.schemas` and fails if any hashed model carries a float-typed epoch field, a string field whose validator accepts an ISO-8601 spelling, or an epoch expressed as anything but `Epoch`; asserts `Epoch.scale` is the single-member literal; asserts `Epoch.seconds_past_j2000.magnitude` satisfies ADR-001's decimal grammar and round-trips decimal to float64 to decimal to within 1 ULP; asserts `Duration` and `Epoch` are not assignable to each other.
2. **`test_geometry_request_has_no_defaults`** (unit tier; **first green by week 1**): constructing a `GeometryRequest` without `frame`, `aberration`, `target` or `observer` raises, and no field of the model declares a default; the `quantity_class` consistency rule rejects `+S` on `range` and `light_time`, and rejects a non-`+S` or `NONE` member on `direction` when `rationale` is empty. A companion AST lint fails on any literal aberration string (`"LT"`, `"LT+S"`, `"CN+S"`, and the rest of the enum) appearing anywhere under `src/farsight/` outside the module that defines the `Aberration` literal, which is what mechanically prevents a convention from being re-introduced as a constant.
3. **`test_time_scale_crosscheck`** (container leg, needs the `spice` extra; **first green by week 2**): SPICE and astropy convert the same UTC literals to TDB seconds across the benchmark span and must agree to a declared tolerance carrying a `# tol:` rationale (`test_tolerance_rationale`, defined in ADR-008); the LSK used is the one pinned in the fixture `KernelSet` (ADR-016). A disagreement fails the build rather than picking a winner.
4. **`ci-geometry-crosscheck`** (defined here; container leg, needs the `spice` extra; **first green by week 2**, which is what the weeks 1-2 exit gate dates it to): runs AT-11's two comparisons — SPICE versus Horizons range against the archived, hash-pinned Horizons response, and SPICE versus astropy station elevation — and fails outside the plan's stated tolerances. It additionally **logs, as a required job artifact, three deltas that this record leaves UNVERIFIED**: the same elevation computed in `ITRF93` versus `IAU_EARTH`; the same range computed under `LT` versus `CN`; and the same state expressed in `J2000` versus astropy's ICRS. The numbers are written back into this record's Confidence table when they exist. A measurement with no scheduled date is an intention, so it has one.
5. **`test_iers_autodownload_disabled`** (unit tier; **first green by week 1**): importing `farsight.units.time` sets `astropy.utils.iers.conf.auto_download` to `False` and `auto_max_age` to `None`, and an AST lint fails on any use of an astropy API requiring UT1 anywhere under `src/farsight/` outside `analysis/`. This is the FarSight-side half of ADR-012's network ban for a base-install dependency; the socket-level half is `test-no-sockets` (defined in ADR-012).
6. **Freeze-time epoch conversion record** (**first green by week 2**, with `test_freeze_protocol`, defined in ADR-001): freezing an object whose epochs were authored in UTC writes an `epoch_conversions` entry per field into the provenance block, naming the field path, the literal and the `lsk_sha256`; freezing with no LSK in the design's `KernelSet` fails and names the missing kernel (ADR-016).
7. **`test_epoch_labels_render`** (**first green by week 4**, with the first package builder): `experiment/epoch_labels.json` contains an entry for every `Epoch` reachable from the package; re-rendering `report/summary.md` from package JSON alone, on a zero-extras install, reproduces the recorded digest byte for byte (ADR-007 item 6 owns the re-render job; this test owns the label half); and `verify` on a zero-extras install reports `epoch_conversions: unchecked (spice extra absent)` rather than passing silently.
8. **PARTIALLY MECHANIZED: TIME-1** — whether the declared frame and aberration correction are the *right* ones for the observable a channel claims to represent. Items 2 and 4 mechanize that a convention was declared, that it is internally consistent with the `quantity_class`, and that the numbers agree with an independent implementation and an external golden; none of them can decide that "this channel is a downlink range at reception" is a true description of what the metric consumes, because that is a claim about physics stated in prose. Residue: the correspondence between a declared convention and the observable, and between our convention and the one the cited referent or golden source used. Review-checklist item **TIME-1** — *"the frame and aberration correction declared for this geometry request match the observable it claims to represent, and match the convention stated in the cited referent or golden source"* — substitutes, and lands as a row in `review_signoffs` on the frozen `ExperimentDesign` and on every `EvidencePackage` containing a geometry channel (ADR-000, ADR-007). The mechanized half is **first green by week 1** for the consistency rule and **week 2** for the cross-check job; the sign-off gate inside `farsight evidence verify` (defined in ADR-007) is **first green by week 4**.
